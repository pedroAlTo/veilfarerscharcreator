## 2. Latest Change — Optional Recommended Ship Fusion Restored

The 17 active blocks were checked against the supplied **Corebook 11.35**, **Keeper’s Companion 11.33**, and **Rust and Steel 11.33** artifacts. Established narrative decisions were preserved.

### The Ship That Learns is a real ending

The Queen’s fusion with a vessel must remain one of the adventure’s full possible endings. It is now presented as **optional and recommended for the Keeper** rather than reduced to an incidental contamination note.

- The Keeper decides whether **The Ship That Learns** is available, preferably before revealing the sliver.
- It is recommended when the adventure opens a continuing campaign and the table welcomes biological-ship themes, an evolving relationship with its first vessel, and a strong Kurogane hook.
- The crew can still prevent it: the sliver must survive, reach a physical live route established in play, and enter a vessel.
- If the enabled ending is achieved, fusion begins and becomes a genuine campaign fact. The ship heals, reroutes, listens, and may eventually recognise the crew as family.
- Before deep integration, the crew may contain or remove the sliver. After completion, separation becomes a dangerous Long-Term Project or campaign objective rather than routine maintenance; it is not impossible by Keeper decree.
- The fusion never transfers legal ownership, automatically controls the ship, or dictates the crew’s emotions.
- The bond currently has no automatic numerical benefit. **Pearl-Thrift remains excluded** because Corebook 11.35 states that each Veil jump consumes one Quantum Pearl. A later mechanical benefit requires an authored and audited Ship Feature.

This distinction is mandatory: **optional** describes the Keeper’s campaign choice and the crew’s ability to prevent the route; it does not make the fusion a lesser or incomplete ending.

### Scene 4 Queen encounter

The user’s map-anchored Queen encounter was integrated into the current Scene 4 after a canon pass.

- The lair is anchored to Map Plate 5, room 18, with the Observation Galleries, Hidden Monitoring Booth, Maintenance Bypass, Power-Routing Manifold, and Energy-Transfer Chamber used as real tactical positions.
- Reading the Queen communicates state rather than language. Sleeping characters can participate fully and are never pressured to Wake.
- The visible four-segment **Hunger** clock advances only when the crew removes food, harms a Weaver, disrupts the feed, collapses a deception, or resolves a directly relevant Failure with Fear in a way that worsens the nest. It never advances for unrelated bad luck or passing time.
- A full Hunger clock reveals the Queen’s defensive intention and returns the decision to the crew. It does not force combat automatically.
- The five approaches remain equally available: soothe, redirect, distract, deceive, force, combine, or invent another answer.
- **Take It by Force** now uses an adventure-specific reskin of the printed **Rust-King Tier 1 phased archetype**. Its phase HP, Evasion, Armor, damage, Continuous rider, Mook, and escape DC come from *Keeper’s Companion 11.33*, Chapter 12. The obsolete Charmed-based Queen profile is explicitly excluded.
- The Phase Two Mercy Window remains open. Removing the cause of the attack can end the fight without killing the Queen.
- The escaping sliver is an optional aftermath, not an automatic third phase. It has no plot armor and uses a three-segment causal chase clock tied to exact rooms on Plate 5.
- A sliver reaches a ship only through a physical live route established in play. If the Keeper enabled **The Ship That Learns**, fusion is a complete possible ending and the recommended continuing-campaign hook.
- The submitted **Pearl-Thrift** benefit was removed from active mechanics. Corebook 11.35, Chapters 17 and 19, states that each Veil jump consumes one Quantum Pearl and that there are no shortcuts or alternative drives. Any future free-jump benefit requires a separately authored and audited Ship Feature.
- Scene 7 now inherits the precise Queen, sliver, energy-feed, and structural states. Scene 8 records any biological ship connection and preserves the crew’s ability to accept, contain, study, remove, or seek help with it.

**Authority:** *Keeper’s Companion 11.33*, Chapter 12, **The Galactic Bestiary**, **The Multi-Stage Boss Fight**, Rust-King Tier 1 archetype, and phased-boss workshop; *Corebook 11.35*, Chapter 9, **Difficulty Classes**; Chapters 17 and 19, Pearl Drive and Veil-jump procedures.

### Preserved lesson structure

Scene 8’s standoff remains a mandatory no-roll lesson. The optional recommended fusion adds an epilogue and campaign hook when achieved; it does not replace the standoff or occur without the required route.

The **“I Need an Idea!” Protocol** remains a terminal backup taught in Scene 2 and available again in Scene 7 only after resolved consequences eliminate every conventional route.

### Previous final-pass rulings retained

### Wealth and mission payment

Scene 1 no longer raises characters from “Wealth Level 0 to 1.” The canonical term is **Wealth Tier**, and a temporary operating advance does not change a character’s economic position.

The advance now:

- pays ordinary passage, provisions, services, and mission costs;
- may contribute to a deposit or leverage;
- does not make a ship a routine purchase;
- does not change personal Wealth Tier.

The 15–35 Credit reward is explicitly a **shared crew payout**, not an amount paid to every character. Credits remain liquid assets; the payment does not automatically increase personal Wealth Tier or Crew Wealth.

**Authority:** *Corebook 11.35*, Chapter 15, **The Wealth Tier System**, **Credits**, and **Crew Wealth**.

### Ship profiles

The purchased vessel and *The Collector* now use the **Tier 2 Cargo Hauler** profile from *Corebook 11.35*, Chapter 17. “Current standard Tier 3 light freighter” was removed because the current book identifies a Tier 2 Cargo Hauler as the worked light-freighter baseline and gives the broader Cargo Hauler frame a Tier 2–3 range.

### Canonical Traits and DCs

Obsolete subtraits were removed:

- `Finesse (Tinker)` became **Finesse** for physical manipulation or **Knowledge (Tech)** for electronic intrusion;
- `Finesse (Pilot)` became **Finesse (Pilot/Navigate)**.

Scene 1’s undefined “moderate Difficulty” became **DC 15 (Challenging)** when a roll is genuinely required. Existing leverage can still remove the roll or change its stakes.

**Authority:** *Corebook 11.35*, Chapter 9, **Subtraits** and **Difficulty Classes**.

### Stabilisation

Scene 7 now states the complete current procedure: an adjacent helper may use **Knowledge (Medicine) or Finesse**, DC 15 while the target is Dying and DC 20 after the target enters Final.

**Authority:** *Corebook 11.35*, Chapter 10, **HP, Dying, and Death**.

### Hunger Weaver compatibility correction

The Hunger Weaver profile embedded in *Keeper’s Companion 11.33* still names the obsolete **Fascinated** Condition. Active Scenes 3 and 6 explicitly replace that result in **Hypnotic Illusions** with the canonical **Stunned** Condition and use the normal Corebook duration and clearing rule.

This is a compatibility correction required by the current Corebook Condition list, not a newly invented mechanic.

**Authority:** *Keeper’s Companion 11.33*, embedded Hunger Weaver profile; *Corebook 11.35*, Chapter 10, **Standard Conditions: Stunned**.

### Weaver Queen profile ruling

The older embedded Weaver Queen profile uses obsolete **Charmed** material and remains excluded. The active adventure now supplies a complete local combat procedure by reskinning the printed Tier 1 Rust-King phased archetype. This is an adventure profile, not a new universal reproductive-caste Weaver entry.

### Clock causality and “I Need an Idea!”

Scene 2 now states that it requires **no tension clock**. A Keeper who elects to add one must name it, show it, and state its triggers. A clock advances only when the fiction causally supports one of those triggers—not because Fear dominated or an unrelated roll failed.

The protocol remains terminal-only:

- not for ordinary indecision;
- not for one failed roll;
- not for one advancing clock;
- not for compassion;
- not for a dead end manufactured to demonstrate the rule.

It begins only when resolved consequences, including completed clocks if the scene actually used them, eliminate every conventional action. It does not rewind clocks, restore destroyed resources, or erase established costs.

**Authority:** *Corebook 11.35*, Chapter 17, **The “I Need an Idea!” Protocol**; *Keeper’s Companion 11.33*, Chapter 4, **Clocks**.

### Scene 8 no-roll standoff preserved

The final standoff remains a mandatory no-roll lesson whenever Silk can intervene. Put the dice away for the social decision. A player may state or summarize the argument; do not grade player eloquence and do not call for Persuasion.

Powers and abilities may change established facts. Dice return for violence or another uncertain action outside the final social decision. If Silk cannot participate, the corresponding final reckoning with the surviving claimant remains a no-roll decision.

## 5. Narrative Identity and Required Lessons

*Hearts in the Void* is an introductory campaign opener for roughly four to six players, suitable for two 3–4 hour sessions or one 6–8 hour session. It teaches crew bonds, investigation, Spotlight, combat, Reputation, moral consequences, possible Awakening, and ship ownership.

### Characters begin Sleeping

- Sleeping characters are complete and capable.
- Awakening is an individual decision, not a group vote or mandatory improvement.
- A custom Awakened character may play, but does not awaken again.
- The adventure places something new within reach; each player decides what their character does with it.

### Everyone can die

No NPC has plot armor. Death also cannot be imposed merely to manufacture drama. An avoidable death produced by play matters more than a required sacrifice.

### Nothing happens on autopilot

An NPC attack, betrayal, escape, confession, or sacrifice is an intention if the crew does not change the situation. Korren’s betrayal can be prevented, delayed, or transformed. Silk can be negotiated with. Zara can change her plan. ARIA-7 can be saved.

### NPCs need the PCs

NPCs ask, need, offer, threaten, or depend. They do not solve the conflict while the players watch. If an NPC resolves the central problem or advances the plot alone, the crew has become an audience.

### “I Need an Idea!” is a terminal backup

Scene 2 introduces the protocol. Scene 7 may use it again only if all conventional actions have genuinely been eliminated by resolved play. The protocol never rewards bad luck by itself and never rewinds the fiction.

### The no-roll standoff is intentional

Do not convert Scene 8’s final social decision into a Persuasion check. It is part of the adventure’s teaching structure.

## 6. Ships and End-State Rules

The crew should normally finish with meaningful access to a first ship, but no route is canonical and ownership is never automatic.

Developed routes include:

- *The Collector* heist;
- purchase with a real debt, sponsor, sacrifice, or financial consequence;
- passage linked to restoring power in the Research Labs;
- the Iron Hearts vessel after enough energy is genuinely recovered or redirected;
- a crew-owned or recovered ship created by play;
- a conditional Joric recovery craft;
- Zara’s vessel only when the achieved relationship and outcome justify it.

Scene 8 distinguishes control, possession, passage, salvage, shared command, and legal ownership. Do not collapse these into “the crew gets the ship.”
