Nine peoples · countless lives

Never a monoculture.

An Accord exchanged. A machine kept alive. A pattern deliberately concealed. These glimpses of daily life show cultures through their practices, shared spaces and choices; no single scene defines an entire people.

01 · Ailuran / Accords and exchanges

The Economy of Truth

Secrets circulate through carefully worded Accords. Reliability gives information its value; the bonds people choose can be as binding as any contract.

02 · Bloomstrider / Cultivation and memory

A memory still growing

A community grows through seasons, tending both its future and the memory held in root and ring. Decisions reach beyond the lives of those who make them.

03 · Celestiari / Listening and remembrance

The work of remembrance

Long memory makes listening a responsibility. Their archives and conversations preserve lived experience, including the grief that public records leave out.

04 · Ethereal / Presence and shared space

Rooms between states

Being present need not mean occupying a single state. Everyday spaces and social habits reflect a people comfortable with uncertainty and different points of view.

05 · Human / Improvisation and connection

Built through belonging

Trade routes, mixed crews and improbable settlements grow through personal ties and improvised solutions. There is no single human way to build a life.

06 · Kryllian / Forge-Clans and craft

Maintenance as devotion

Making and maintaining are moral work. A well-kept machine carries a Forge-Clan’s reputation, its obligations and its refusal to surrender useful things to decay.

07 · Kryx / Discipline and belonging

The pack's compact

The pack is a social compact built on competence and mutual obligation. Planning, sharing and earned trust matter as much as the hunt itself.

08 · Rift Spinner / Identity and privacy

The choice to be known

Every individual bears a distinctive pattern across abdomen and torso, sometimes extending to legs and face. Clothing can conceal those markings: being recognized is not always an invitation.

09 · Synthetic / Autonomy and memory

A self chosen

A manufactured origin does not decide a person’s purpose. Self-repair, personal memory and the freedom to choose a body express lives made on their own terms.

To catalogue a people is to decide what the archive believes matters.
