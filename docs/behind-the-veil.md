# Behind the Veil

The public design journal lives at `/behind-the-veil/`. Each article has a stable, separately shareable URL. The first is `/behind-the-veil/overkill.html`.

The Overkill article is edited in English from the author's Spanish notes supplied on 19 September 2026: personal-scale damage did not express the range of advanced destructive technology the setting needed; an open-ended threshold ladder connects the fiction to consequences; five-point increments target a more lethal tone, ten-point increments preserve heroic latitude. No unreported prototypes, dates or playtest findings have been invented.

Rule reference: Corebook 12.06, pp. 94–95. Standard adds one HP for every full ten damage above Severe; Deadly uses five. The author clarified armor spending after reviewing this article: each spent slot removes exactly 1 HP, with multiple slots usable on a hit. The workbench uses Tactical Armor (Corebook 12.06, p. 125): five slots and base Major 10 / Severe 20, with a level-2 wearer adding 2 to both thresholds (p. 123). Five gold, intact shields start available (`aria-pressed=true`); spending one darkens it and reveals two separated, cracked halves. A second click restores it for experimentation. Each shield’s accessible label gives its state and available action. The remaining count starts at 5 / 5 ready. Tactical and Reflective features are outside this damage-only example. HP loss cannot fall below zero. The article and source note distinguish this clarification from the PDF wording. Its example fixes Major 12 / Severe 22 and excludes special effects and naval-scale procedures. The formula is open-ended; the slider is a bounded example.

The source-system comparison acknowledges Daggerheart's optional Massive Damage and fiction-first rulings, with a primary-source link to SRD 2.0 pp. 3 and 50. The article presents the author's setting-specific design goal, not a claim that fantasy has no large-scale threats or that Daggerheart lacks rulings for them.

Public entry points: home design-journal feature, main navigation on the home/book pages, Corebook's combat tile, sitemap. Each article has Open Graph metadata and a copy-link control. Copying on a deploy preview retains the preview host so a link never promises an unmerged production page. No posts or messages are sent to itch.io or TTRPG Design.

To add an article, add a static HTML page to the folder using the existing navigation/footer, a unique title/description/canonical and source references. Add a card to the index and an entry to sitemap.xml. Keep author recollection separate from rule text and clearly identified examples. Static article content and the example table remain available without JavaScript.

Editorial status: the current article is provisional. The author will supply the definitive Spanish explanation. Translate that text into English while preserving their first-person voice, rhythm, examples and qualifications. Keep optional editorial suggestions separate; do not invent design history or playtest evidence.

## Drones · 26 September 2026

The second article is available as English `/behind-the-veil/drones.html` and Spanish `/behind-the-veil/drones-es.html`. Both retain the author's first-person design account supplied on 26 September: pilots beyond vehicles; modular chassis and modules; the Cyberpunk spotlight problem; fragility and traceability; weapon damage independent of character Proficiency; signal coverage as a group planning opportunity; shared control through a neural link costing 1 cr and no module slots.

Edit `docs/drones-article-copy.json` and run `node scripts/build-drone-article.mjs` to regenerate both complete static HTML pages. Language links preserve section anchors. Each language has its own canonical, alternate language metadata and sitemap entry. No-JavaScript readers retain the full article and illustrations.

The pixel styling builds on Overkill. `drone-scenes.mjs` animates supplied sprite frames only while scenes are visible; page visibility, reduced motion and the reader's pause control stop the loop. Signal choices and class spotlight controls illustrate the author's ideas without calculating combat or construction statistics. The scene of a discovered drone aligns the weapon, muzzle and shot to the soldier's body at each viewport size.

BarelyApes is credited for the acquired toolkit sprites and props. The author-supplied spider uses the accompanying frames guide; its individual creator was not established from the asset metadata, so the page does not attribute it to BarelyApes. Source PNGs remain unchanged, with transparent padding handled in CSS.

## Space combat · 6 October 2026

The third article is available in English at `/behind-the-veil/space-combat.html` and Spanish at `/behind-the-veil/space-combat-es.html`. The copy preserves the author's account of Spotlight-based stations, directional shield management, Heat, Mount Rating and the “I need an idea!” protocol. Both versions link to Corebook 12.06, Chapter 17, pp. 158–165 and identify the arcade encounter as an illustration of those rules.

Edit `scripts/build-space-combat-article.mjs` and run `node scripts/build-space-combat-article.mjs` to regenerate both pages. The R-Type-style scene uses the licensed, credited Raphael Gonçalves spaceship sprites. It starts with small craft and advances through a Corvette, Frigate, Heavy Cruiser and Dreadnought. Weapon damage uses the printed weapon expression and the firing hull's Mount Rating; shield arcs map Fore/Aft/Port/Starboard to front/rear/above/below, consume Armor Slots before Hull, and feed overflow into the printed Hull-damage bands. The simulated player ship is explicitly labelled a Corvette-profile fighter: Tier 2, MR ×2, Hull 22, six slots per arc, Heat Cap 12 and Evasion 12. Mobile controls, keyboard controls, reduced-motion behavior, pausing and restart are supported.

The 7 October revision uses the shared Overkill pixel typography/panels and only existing ship, character, console and projectile sprites for illustrations. The copied ship PNGs have their connected flat background removed and transparent margins cropped; original source sprites remain unchanged. Shield effects use four continuous sections fitted closely around the supplied ship silhouette that change from white through blue, violet and yellow to red as they weaken, disappear at zero, and recharge automatically. The arcade maps a round to six seconds and regenerates the printed two slots per arc on that timer, including between waves; this is demonstration timing, not a new rule for round length. Mouse movement steers the ship; holding the mouse or dragging a finger also fires. Coordinates respect canvas scaling, touch cancellation releases fire, and keyboard/button controls remain available. Regression coverage includes the outside-panel pause control that formerly prevented startup and the player-damage target that formerly stopped the render loop on the first hit.

### Drones scene corrections · 27 September 2026

The modular infiltration scene uses the author's mechanical recolor, `spider-drone.png`, with the original 64px frame stride retained after the supplied sheet's trim. Its facing follows rightward movement and upward climbing. The scout's light cone follows the paired headlights and their four-frame bob, including mirrored flight.

Signal now uses `drone-signal.mjs`, verified against Corebook 12.06 pp. 137–138 and 140: Stable, Unstable and Lost, narrative range bands, active jamming, allowed channels and surviving relay support. The flight crosses a declared protected area at Far; distance plus jamming loses the link at Very Far and triggers the example's Freeze protocol. Readers can scrub range or compare preparation choices. The meter never invents a percentage or a distance in metres.

The shared-control scene uses a smaller drone inside a ceiling duct, with the four characters hidden behind the acquired cargo chest. Its model in `drone-neural-puzzle.mjs` retains completed milestones until the reader explicitly restarts. The page gives no solution description: feedback comes from localized thoughts aligned with the selected portrait and dialogue anchored to the guard. Only the character selector has a spotlight. Engineering actions draw a signal to the affected device. After the duct, the first engineering attempt opens the guarded door. The guard reacts, closes it at the panel, returns to his post, and only then comments about the wind. The next engineering attempt connects the encrypted radio channel, with a persistent localized connection indicator. Each dismissal chooses one of three localized departure remarks and holds it while the guard walks away. The ceiling duct continues beyond both scene edges. Its floor grille above the guarded doorway opens at completion: the drone descends through it and follows the four crew members inside, then the grille closes. Completed departures remain finished when motion is re-enabled; restart restores the closed grille and all actors. Both languages remain playable with reduced motion; completed outcomes are announced accessibly and the radio exchange remains readable.

Shield impacts are traced against that same compact outline using relative projectile/ship motion during the frame; the affected arc is selected at first contact, rather than when the enemy fires. The four HUD tiles also reinforce their own arc, via click/tap or keys 1–4 (front, rear, above, below); +2 slots costs +2 Heat, with no charge for an already full arc. Floating labels distinguish shield loss from Hull loss. Separate Player and Keeper panels inside the game frame show projected 3D d6/d8/d10/d12 dice using the actual individual rolled values, modifier, Mount Rating and final damage. On mobile they sit side by side and allow pointer input through to the game.
